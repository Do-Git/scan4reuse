# Website structure and navigation

Reviewed on 2 November 2026 for Scan4Reuse's Switzerland-wide, two-sided reuse and delivery service.

## Sources

- [NN/g: Homepage Design — 5 Fundamental Principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/) recommends explaining the company and its offering clearly, using representative examples and making important tasks easy to find.
- [NN/g: Menu-Design Checklist](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/menu-design/) recommends familiar, descriptive labels, scannable menus, adequate touch targets and visible desktop navigation.
- [NN/g: Top 3 IA Questions about Navigation Menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ia-questions-navigation-menus/) explains that category count and order depend on the site's content and users' needs; there is no universal sequence for every business.
- [Unbounce: The Anatomy of a Landing Page](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-articles/the-anatomy-of-a-landing-page/) describes a clear proposition, useful examples, benefits and a focused action, with a closing invitation to act. This is homepage guidance adapted to a company site, rather than a reason to remove its navigation.
- [Yale Usability & Digital Accessibility: Less is More](https://usability.yale.edu/ux/best-practices/less-is-more) recommends grouping related content, reducing distractions, using clear category labels and emphasizing the primary action.

## Scan4Reuse's implementation

The chosen sequence is an application of those principles to this site. It is not a claim that this exact order has been proven to produce the best conversion rate.

Primary navigation on desktop and mobile:

1. **How it works** — replaces the less specific “The journey”.
2. **Services** — covers connected logistics and the adjacent scanning, marketplace and report overview.
3. **Pricing** — both separate 8% transaction fees and the CHF 5/m² report rate, below the service explanation.
4. **Impact** — mission, ESG commitments and the five SDGs.
5. **About us** — the company's origin and the actual founding team.
6. **Plan a delivery** — the primary action; visually distinguished from informational links on mobile.

Homepage reading order:

1. Company introduction, Swiss coverage and labelled example.
2. How the client-to-partner workflow works.
3. Connected logistics, followed immediately by the supporting services and tools.
4. Pricing and independent calculators.
5. Impact and SDGs.
6. Company origin and team.
7. Common questions about eligibility, fees, adjustments and documents.
8. The full client/partner delivery planner.
9. A closing delivery action and consistent footer navigation.

This lets visitors understand the offer and costs before reaching the full form. Ready visitors can jump directly to the planner from the header or introduction. Prices remain off the opening screen, as requested, and remain visible on the homepage without navigating to another page. Both transaction calculators retain the CHF 1,000 default.

The detailed pricing page uses the same navigation terms and order. The delivery-document workspace provides explicit routes home and back to the planner. Existing `#logistics`, `#ecosystem`, `#story`, `#team` and `#delivery` links remain available alongside the new `#services` and `#about` destinations.

All existing team photographs, SDG icons, quote mechanics, adjusted delivery-fee calculations and PDF exports are preserved. Company information supplies factual context; no customer testimonials or adoption metrics have been invented.
